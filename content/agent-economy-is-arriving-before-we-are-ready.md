# The agent economy is arriving before trust is ready

I want AI agents that help with ordinary work.

An assistant can gather sources, draft something useful, compare options, update a system, prepare a purchase, or turn messy notes into something I can review. That interests me more than a suspiciously perfect demo where everyone ignores the seven failed attempts.

The value is less tab-hopping and admin sludge. Less "I know what I want, but I now have to spend forty minutes arranging the software around the thing I want."

Agents are moving beyond chat boxes. They are going to touch money, files, tools, creative software, shopping carts, calendars, customer systems, and trading rails. The latest official releases from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are all framed around work loops rather than just answers. Stripe is already pushing payment infrastructure that can support agent-mediated buying flows. Research is appearing around language-model agents operating under real capital. Creative assistants are moving into design and production tools.

That is useful, and it makes me twitchy.

## Users need to see the boundaries

"AI agents are too autonomous" is too blunt a diagnosis. My concern is that agents gain the ability to act before users get clear controls over those actions.

If an agent can do something on my behalf, I need to understand five things quickly:

1. What can it do?
2. What can it not do?
3. What has it already done?
4. What will it ask me before doing?
5. How do I undo or stop it?

Most agent demos answer the first question well and stay vague on the other four. They do not always show the boundaries.

That is the bit I care about most. Boundaries are not anti-automation. They are what make automation usable for normal people who cannot spend all day reading logs.

## Payment changes the whole feeling

An agent that can buy a product asks for more trust than one that can recommend it.

That does not mean agent payments are bad. In fact, I can see the appeal immediately. If I trust a system, why should it not renew a domain, buy printer ink, top up a subscription, book the cheapest sensible travel option, or handle boring purchases within a budget?

The catch is that "within a budget" is doing a lot of work.

I would want rules like:

- never spend over a set amount without approval
- never start a subscription without approval
- never buy from unknown merchants without approval
- show me the final basket before checkout
- keep a receipt I can understand
- let me cancel the action easily

That sounds obvious, but a lot of tech fails at obvious once growth pressure arrives. The temptation will be to make the agent feel smoother by removing friction. Sometimes that will be helpful. Sometimes the friction is the safety rail.

## The trust layer needs to be visible

The best agent systems will probably feel less magical than the worst demos.

They will show drafts. They will show receipts. They will say when evidence is weak. They will separate "I can prepare this" from "I can send this". They will let you approve a class of low-risk actions while still stopping on anything expensive, public, or hard to reverse.

That is what I want from my own workflow as well. I do not want agents publishing posts, sending newsletters, spending money, or changing websites without review. Drafts, queues and approval gates let me review the work and keep control of it.

I think users feeling properly in control will matter more than which model is cleverest over the next year.

## My current test

If I am evaluating an agent product now, I am less impressed by "look what it can do" than I used to be.

My first questions are:

- Can I see what it touched?
- Can I see why it chose that action?
- Can I stop it?
- Can I limit it?
- Can I review before anything public happens?
- Can I undo the result?

If the answer is no, then the agent is still a demo to me, even if it looks polished.

I think agents will be useful. I expect the winning products to explain their limits rather than suggest agents can do anything. They should make it clear what an agent is allowed to do, what it has done, and where the human still has the final word. That is the ambition I trust.
