## Summary

On 19 October 2026 Albertans were asked whether Alberta should remain a province of Canada or start the legal process towards a binding referendum on separation [1]. CBC News projected a "remain" win early on 20 October [2]. Unofficial figures with 91.42% of results reported showed 1,472,115 votes (75.18%) for remain and 485,933 (24.82%) for a further referendum [1]. Elections Alberta says official results will be published on 29 October 2026 [3].

## Background

Alberta's Citizen Initiative Act lets citizens force a referendum by petition [1]. Competing petitions were organised: one by the pro-Canada "Forever Canadian" campaign led by Thomas Lukaszuk, and one by separatist groups [1][2].

In May 2026 the Court of King's Bench of Alberta struck down the separatist petition, after arguments that Indigenous peoples had not been properly consulted on a change that would affect treaty rights [1]. Elections Alberta verified the pro-Canada petition at over 400,000 signatures and the separatist petition at almost 223,000 [1].

On 21 May 2026 Premier Danielle Smith announced that a remain-or-separate question would be added to nine other referendum questions [1]. A vote for the second option would not itself have taken Alberta out of Canada. It would have started a process towards a later binding vote, and the federal Clarity Act sets out how Parliament assesses any such question and result [1][5].

Separatist campaigners argued that Ottawa had treated Alberta unfairly for years and that federal environmental policy held back its oil and gas industry [2]. Federalist campaigners warned of significant economic risks from separation [2].

## Timeline

- **2000:** Parliament passes the Clarity Act, which governs how the House of Commons considers a provincial secession question [5].
- **13 May 2026:** The Court of King's Bench of Alberta strikes down the separatist petition [1].
- **21 May 2026:** Premier Smith announces the remain-or-separate question [1].
- **19 October 2026:** Referendum day; Elections Alberta reports advance and special-ballot turnout of 43.1% before polls close [3].
- **20 October 2026:** CBC News projects a remain win [2]. Premier Smith says Alberta "will remain a province of Canada" and that she will not hold another referendum on the issue while she is premier [4].
- **29 October 2026:** Official results due [3].

## Key players

- **Danielle Smith:** Premier of Alberta and United Conservative Party (UCP) leader; said from the start she would vote to remain [2][4].
- **Mark Carney:** Prime Minister of Canada; welcomed the result in a statement on 20 October [2].
- **Thomas Lukaszuk:** founder of Forever Canadian, the pro-Canada petition campaign [2].
- **Keith Wilson:** co-leader of the pro-independence group Let Alberta Decide [2].
- **Elections Alberta:** the independent body that runs the vote and publishes official results [3].

## Why it matters

The result settles the question for now: the premier has said there will be no further separation referendum while she leads the province [4]. She also said her government will keep pressing for more provincial autonomy within Canada's constitution [4].

Around a quarter of voters chose the separation route, and separatist leaders say their campaign will continue [2]. CBC reports that polls suggested a slight majority of UCP voters favoured that option, which leaves a divide inside the governing party [2].

## What is not yet known

- Final, certified figures, due on 29 October [3].
- Results on the nine other questions, covering immigration and constitutional changes, which were still being counted on 20 October [2][4].
