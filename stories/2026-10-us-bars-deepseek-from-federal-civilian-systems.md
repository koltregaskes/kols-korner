## Summary

On 20 October 2026 the Federal Register published a Department of Homeland Security (DHS) notice of an order that excludes products and services from Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd from federal civilian procurement [1]. The same order requires civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek products, and third-party products built using DeepSeek implementations, from their IT systems [1]. China's foreign ministry said it opposes "overstretching the concept of national security" [3].

## Background

DeepSeek is a Chinese AI company. In January 2025 the release of its low-cost AI assistant set off a sell-off in US technology shares, and Nvidia lost about $593 billion of market value in one day, a record for a Wall Street company at the time [4].

Weeks later, in February 2025, the security firm NowSecure published an audit reporting multiple security and privacy flaws in the DeepSeek iOS app [5]. The DHS order cites that audit among its sources [2].

The order uses powers in the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018 (41 U.S.C. 1321-1328), following a review of a recommendation from the Federal Acquisition Security Council [1].

## Timeline

- **27 January 2025:** DeepSeek's AI assistant triggers a US tech sell-off; Nvidia records a one-day loss of about $593 billion in market value [4].
- **6 February 2025:** NowSecure publishes its audit of the DeepSeek iOS app [5].
- **24 July 2026:** Original release date of DHS Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01 [2].
- **20 October 2026:** The Federal Register publishes the DHS notice of the order (docket DHS-2026-0628) [1].
- **20 October 2026:** At its regular briefing, China's foreign ministry says it will "resolutely safeguard" Chinese companies' lawful rights and interests [3].

## Key players

- **US Department of Homeland Security:** issued the order; the CISA Cybersecurity Division runs its implementation team [1].
- **Office of Management and Budget:** copied on the order [2].
- **Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd:** the company whose products are excluded, together with its affiliates [1].
- **China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs:** spokesperson Mao Ning responded on 20 October [3].

## Why it matters

The order reaches beyond DeepSeek's own apps to third-party products and services "developed using DeepSeek implementations" [1]. Agencies and their suppliers may therefore need to check whether tools they use are built on DeepSeek models [1].

It applies to federal civilian executive branch agencies, except for their national security systems and secure compartmented information systems, which fall under other authorities [2].

## What is not yet known

- The public notice does not say when agencies must complete removal; the full order is in DHS docket DHS-2026-0628 on regulations.gov [1].
- Neither cited source reports a response from DeepSeek itself [1][3].
